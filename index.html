How the picture gets made

  1. The camera sits exactly in line with the finish line and looks across the track. Its sensor is a single column of pixels, so it sees the line and nothing either side of it.
  2. It reads that column again and again. Real systems take 10,000 or more readings per second, each 1 pixel wide. Here it is 200 per second and each reading is several pixels wide so you can see it.
  3. Each new reading is placed next to the previous one. A rider shows up in the photo only while some part of them is on the line, and the photo's horizontal axis becomes a clock.
  4. Judges place a cursor on the leading edge of the front tyre and read the time straight off the image.

Why the bikes look wrong

  • The backdrop and the track never move, so every reading repeats the same pixels. They turn into horizontal streaks.
  • Width in the photo is how long something spent on the line. A faster rider looks shorter. Drag the scan line width to stretch or squash everyone.
  • The top of a wheel moves at twice the bike's speed and the bottom is momentarily still. So spokes curve and the wheel leans.
